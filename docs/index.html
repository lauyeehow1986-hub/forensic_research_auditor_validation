Why Do 79.27% of Data-Related Summary Tables Pass Math? The 4 Anatomies of Real-World Data Inconsistencies:

1. The "Excel Reality" (Formula Generation):
When synthetic or simulated patient rows are generated in spreadsheets using =AVERAGE() and =STDEV(), the resulting summary numbers are mathematically self-consistent. Summary tables cannot expose raw-row synthesis without raw line-level audit.
2. Continuous Unbounded Assays:
Assays like optical density (OD 450nm), Western blot densitometry, and qPCR fold changes have infinite degrees of freedom; any decimal mean is mathematically possible unless variance bounds are violated.
3. Sample Size Omission:
Many clinical tables report percentages or means without specifying per-group N in the column header, preventing modular tests like GRIM from calculating integer divisibility.
4. Narrative-Only Numerical Reporting (153 Papers):
8.2% of data-related retractions presented numerical findings exclusively in running text or bar charts, with zero formal <table> elements in the XML full text.

Scope Demarcation: Decoupling Pixel Figure Forensics from FastMCP Tabular Math

Specialized Computer Vision Domain

Why Figure Forensics is Decoupled: Western blot duplication, microscopy cloning, and gel lane splicing operate strictly in the pixel image domain. Because the FastMCP math auditor evaluates JATS XML tables and statistical expressions, auditing image pixels belongs to external specialized computer vision pipelines (e.g. Proofig, ImCheck). Confining the table auditor strictly to tabular data eliminates spectrum bias and prevents keyword false positives (such as engineering papers with "image" in their title).

Deterministic Mathematical Axioms & Forensic Sieves

Rigorous division of cognitive labor: zero LLM mental math. All numerical bounds computed deterministically via FastMCP.

1. GRIM: Discrete Integer Granularity (Brown & Heathers, 2016)

Discrete Counts

For discrete integer observations across sample size N and items k, the total sum must be an integer. The reported mean rounded to D decimal places must have an integer sum within the interval under at least one rounding convention (half_to_even, half_up, half_down, truncate).

verify_grim_grimmer_debit(mean, sd, n, decimals_mean=2, decimals_sd=2, items=1, is_binary=False)

2. Analytic-GRIMMER: Standard Deviation Parity (Allard, 2018; Anaya, 2016)

SD Bounds

For any integer sum passing GRIM, the sum of squares must fall within the rounding interval of SD and strictly satisfy the parity identity: sum(x_i^2) = sum(x_i) (mod 2). If parity fails, the standard deviation is mathematically impossible.

3. DEBIT: Binary Summary Standard Deviations (Heathers & Brown, 2019)

Binary Proportions

For binary (0/1) outcomes with proportion p = S/N, the sample standard deviation is mathematically fixed by SD = sqrt((N / (N-1)) * p * (1-p)). Reported SDs outside this envelope represent impossible binary summary data.

4. Carlisle Baseline ANOVA & Stouffer Pooling (Carlisle, 2012, 2017)

Continuous Biomarkers

Across continuous baseline variables, one-way ANOVA p-values are converted to standard normal quantiles z_i and pooled via Stouffer's formula Z_S = sum(z_i) / sqrt(m). Right-tail cumulative probability > 0.999 flags hyper-homogeneous baseline variance compression (unnatural identical trial arms).

simulate_carlisle_stouffer(table_rows=[{"variable": "Age", "means": [55.2, 55.1], "sds": [10.2, 10.3], "ns": [50, 50]}, ...])

5. Benford Scale-Invariance Dynamic Range Rule (Diekmann, 2007; Nigrini, 2012)

False Positive Suppression

Benford's Law mathematically applies only to scale-invariant data spanning multiple orders of magnitude (span = log10(max/min) >= 1.5). Narrow-range physical constants (reaction yields 80%-99%, temperatures, pH, NMR ppm) naturally violate uniformity and must be gated as BENFORD_INAPPLICABLE_NARROW_RANGE to prevent false discoveries.
